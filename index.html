The one rule that keeps this a career and not a crime

Only ever touch systems you own or are explicitly authorised to test. Everything above gives you the exact same skills a real attacker has — enumeration, exploitation, privilege escalation, persistence — legally. When your resume says "I can pentest," the eJPT/CPTS and your lab write-ups are the proof. A hacked Instagram account is not proof; it's a case file with your name on it.

Money-saving reality

Swap CPTS for AZ-500 and you drop the total to ~4,175 AED. Ask Real Secure if they'll sponsor a cert — MSPs often reimburse exams that make staff more billable, and Security+ or AZ-500 is an easy "yes." Never book an exam until you're consistently passing practice tests; a retake is 500–1,500 AED of avoidable pain.

Turning a 2.0 GPA into a master's admit

Straight talk: a 2.0 won't disappear, and some top programs have hard GPA floors you can't argue past. But plenty of good programs weight relevant experience — and that's where your year does the work.

Target the right program type

Prioritise programs that explicitly accept relevant work experience in lieu of GPA — common in the UK, Australia, and many UAE-based master's. Filter for "work experience considered" in admissions criteria rather than chasing pure ranking.

Let certs carry the signal

Security+, eJPT and CPTS are objective, external proof of competence that admissions committees read as "this person can do the work" — exactly the doubt a low GPA raises.

Write the SOP around the arc

Your story writes itself: bad-teaching bachelor's → self-taught practical skills → engineer title → AI-security specialisation in one year. That trajectory is more compelling than a flat 3.5.

Show, don't claim

The GitHub portfolio + pentest report + AI-security project are attachable evidence. A reference letter from Rateesh/Banat at Real Secure closes the loop.

Do this in Q3, not Q4

Email 4–5 target programs' admissions offices directly in months 7–9 and ask plainly: "I have a 2.0 GPA but a year of professional cyber experience and X certifications — would my application be considered?" Their answer tells you exactly where to spend your application effort, before deadlines pressure you.
